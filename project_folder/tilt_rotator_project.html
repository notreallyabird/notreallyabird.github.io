MIT The 77 Lab · Research Project

Excavator Tilt Rotator

A compact, electrically actuated tilt rotator built for a 1/12-scale excavator, bringing more realistic attachments to teleoperation research and operator training.

50° Bucket tilt range
360° Continuous rotation
2.26 N·m Peak output torque measured
1.13 kg Assembly total weight
01 / PROJECT OVERVIEW

More motion at the end of the arm

A conventional excavator bucket mainly curls and uncurls. A tilt rotator adds two more degrees of freedom: the bucket can tilt from side to side and rotate continuously, helping an operator approach a task from different angles without repositioning the excavator.

I worked on a 1/12-scale version for MIT's The 77 Lab. The attachment and integrated grapple are designed to fit a miniature training excavator while supporting research on remote operation, control interfaces, and operator training.

02 / MECHANICAL DESIGN

An electrical differential in a tight envelope

Full-size tilt rotators commonly use hydraulic actuation. At 1/12 scale, reproducing that architecture would add cost and complexity, so the project uses a compact, stepper-driven mechanical differential instead.

Differential motion

Two motor inputs work through a differential gearbox to produce the attachment's tilt and rotation motions. Non-backdrivable worm gear stages help hold the selected position without continuously supplying motor holding current.

Designed around the excavator

The mechanism had to deliver useful torque and motion while staying inside the host machine's small mounting envelope. The mechanical paper reports a maximum height of 85.7 mm, a 135 × 135 mm platform envelope, and a 2 kg final assembly.

01 / TILT

Side-to-side bucket angle

A 150° tilt range gives the operator more freedom to orient the bucket for a task.

02 / ROTATION

Continuous bucket rotation

The attachment can rotate continuously through 360°, rather than stopping at a fixed angle.

03 / GRAPPLE

Interchangeable work

A grapple attachment extends the platform beyond digging, enabling pickup and placement tasks.

03 / ELECTRICAL & CONTROL

Stepper-driven, remotely configurable

Compact motion control

The control system combines NEMA 8 stepper motors with TMC2209 stepper drivers and an ESP32 microcontroller. This compact hardware stack provides independent motor actuation for the attachment's motions.

Built for experimentation

UART communication allows motor operating modes to be adjusted during development. Combined with the worm-gear transmission's resistance to backdriving, the setup supports repeatable motion experiments without relying on continuous holding current.

04 / PERFORMANCE & TESTING

Measured against the design targets

Testing reported in the mechanical design paper showed the final mechanism exceeding its 200 N·cm output-torque design requirement. The assembly also demonstrated the intended range of motion and rotation speed for scaled excavator training and teleoperation research.

226.0 N·cm Peak output torque
53.3% Measured mechanical efficiency
7.5 rpm Continuous rotation speed
200 N·cm Output-torque design requirement

From mechanism to platform

Integration brings the two motorized axes, gearbox, bucket, and excavator mounting interface together as one attachment. The scaled platform offers a lower-cost, repeatable environment for developing and evaluating remote-control methods before they are considered for full-size machinery.
